Methodology

A transparent framework for local specialty demand estimates.

CurisData estimates the outpatient specialty care that residents of a defined market are expected to use in a year.

We analyze individual-level healthcare survey records weighted to statistically represent the U.S. civilian, noninstitutionalized population. The models examine how specialty use varies across demographic characteristics, then apply those relationships to the demographic profile of a specific geography.

The evidence base

CurisData uses healthcare survey evidence that includes people who use specialty care and people who do not. This gives the analysis a population denominator, rather than beginning only with provider records or the patients already observed in a healthcare system.

Representative population evidence

Survey weights support estimates for the U.S. civilian, noninstitutionalized population and reflect the characteristics of people across the country.

Individual-level utilization patterns

The records support analysis of how specialty use differs by age, recorded sex, race and ethnicity, region, income, and other compatible population characteristics.

How local estimates are developed

CurisData does not apply a single national utilization rate to every market. It models the relationship between population characteristics and specialty use, then applies that relationship to the local demographic profile.

  1. Define a market geography, from granular neighborhood areas through ZIP codes, counties, trade areas, or custom geographies.
  2. Describe the compatible demographic profile of the population living in that geography.
  3. Estimate the specialty users and visits that population is expected to generate based on modeled utilization relationships.
  4. Aggregate nonoverlapping areas into the geographic view needed for mapping, reporting, or further analysis.

Local demographic differences matter

Two markets with the same population can generate different levels of specialty utilization. The estimates are designed to make those demographic differences visible in a consistent analytical baseline.

What the data measures

Estimated annual users

Residents expected to have at least one included specialty visit during a year.

Estimated annual visits

Total included specialty visits those residents are expected to generate, including repeat visits.

The data is intended to establish a resident-generated demand baseline. Users across specialties are not unique people and should not be added together as if they were.

Scope and important limits

The current specialty dataset covers 25 categories of office-based and hospital outpatient physician visits. It does not estimate inpatient stays, emergency-department demand, unmet clinical need, provider capacity, or a particular organization’s future volume.

Resident utilization describes where people live, not where they receive care. To assess a particular location or organization, combine the demand baseline with access, capacity, patient travel, referrals, competition, and achievable share.

Using the estimates responsibly

  • Align definitions: Compare like populations, geographies, periods, specialties, and visit settings.
  • Keep source timing visible: The estimates are model-based and should be interpreted with their source periods and demographic inputs.
  • Respect geographic boundaries: Aggregate counts across nonoverlapping areas before calculating rates; overlapping radii cannot be added together.
  • Use other evidence for location decisions: Claims, patient flows, competitive supply, access, and operating assumptions each answer different questions.

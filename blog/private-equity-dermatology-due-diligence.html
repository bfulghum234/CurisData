Diligencing the growth story: specialty demand in healthcare private equity.

Historical performance explains where a multi-location dermatology practice has been. Population-based specialty demand can help an analytics team test whether its market and expansion story is credible.

By CurisData November 3, 2026

The External Market View

Diligence the growth assumptions, not only the historical P&L.

A private-equity diligence process may have detailed financial statements, clinic-level performance, provider rosters, and management forecasts. Those materials describe the organization. They do not independently establish whether the surrounding markets can support the growth plan.

An analytics firm can use CurisData to add a consistent view of resident-generated dermatology users and visits around existing clinics, proposed locations, and potential expansion markets. Combined with operating and competitive evidence, that demand layer can help test the geographic assumptions behind the investment thesis.

Key Takeaway

CurisData can help evaluate the market component of a growth story. It does not validate reimbursement, margins, physician recruitment, operational capacity, transaction value, or investment returns.

Three Diligence Questions

Connect the practice footprint to the markets it serves.

01

How strong is the existing footprint?

Compare clinic performance and patient reach with the resident-generated demand, competition, and network overlap surrounding each location.

02

Does the expansion pipeline make sense?

Apply consistent trade areas and market criteria to proposed clinics instead of accepting each opportunity as a separate narrative.

03

Where could growth come next?

Screen national or regional markets for further investigation, then add supply, access, acquisition, and operating constraints.

Define the dermatology business being evaluated.

“Dermatology” is not one uniform operating model. The American Academy of Dermatology describes care involving skin, hair, and nail conditions as well as cosmetic concerns. A multi-location practice may emphasize medical dermatology, surgical services, Mohs surgery, pediatric care, cosmetic procedures, or a combination.

The diligence brief should identify which services drive visits, revenue, referrals, staffing, and expansion. Population-based physician visits can provide a baseline for specialty utilization, but a general dermatology measure should not be presented as a direct forecast of Mohs cases, cosmetic procedures, or another narrower offering.

The analysis should also identify the purpose of growth. New clinics intended to reach a new population create a different market question from relocations, capacity expansions, acquisitions, or offices designed to support an existing referral network.

Add a consistent demand baseline to every clinic market.

CurisData estimates the annual residents expected to use a physician specialty and the total included visits those residents are expected to generate. The estimates are derived from nationally representative government healthcare-utilization relationships applied to compatible local demographics.

For diligence, the analytics team can aggregate dermatology users and visits to the same catchments used for existing and proposed clinics. The result is not an estimate of clinic encounters. It is an external market baseline that can be compared consistently across the practice’s footprint.

  • Estimated users provide context on the breadth of the resident population expected to receive included dermatology care.
  • Estimated visits reflect the annual utilization generated by those residents, including repeat visits.
  • Compatible rates help distinguish the effect of population size from differences associated with demographic composition.
  • Source and quality context identifies the utilization period, demographic inputs, modeled geography, and available warnings.

Interrogate the existing clinic footprint.

The strongest existing clinics can help the diligence team understand what success looks like for this particular organization. Compare clinic performance, patients, providers, and service mix with the demand and competitive context around each location.

A clinic performing above what its local resident baseline might suggest could be drawing patients from a wider area, benefiting from referrals, serving a differentiated offering, or reflecting data-definition differences. A clinic performing below a favorable market baseline could have capacity, provider, payer, access, execution, or maturity constraints. The variance is a prompt for investigation, not a diagnosis.

Look for concentration risk as well. Several clinics can appear individually attractive while drawing from substantially overlapping populations. Measure the demand within each catchment and the unique demand added by the network, rather than adding overlapping trade-area totals.

Test the management expansion pipeline consistently.

Proposed clinics often arrive with different levels of analysis. One may be supported by a detailed market study, another by broker outreach, and another by management familiarity. A common evaluation framework makes those opportunities comparable.

  • Market demand. Compare dermatology users, visits, rates, and relevant population change within a consistent catchment.
  • Competitive supply. Identify distinct dermatology practices, physicians, and service offerings rather than relying on raw directory records.
  • Network contribution. Estimate the net-new population and demand reached after accounting for existing clinic overlap.
  • Patient and referral evidence. Add claims, patient origins, referring providers, and payer information where available and appropriately used.
  • Execution requirements. Separate market opportunity from the ability to recruit clinicians, build volume, contract with payers, and operate the site.

The output should rank which proposed markets deserve deeper diligence, explain the variables driving the result, and identify the assumptions that could change the ranking.

Translate market demand into explicit scenarios.

Resident-generated visits should not flow directly into a clinic forecast. The team needs an explicit bridge from total market utilization to the portion a clinic might plausibly serve.

Low, base, and high cases can apply different assumptions for achievable share, ramp time, provider availability, visit capacity, service mix, referrals, inbound patients, and competitive response. Existing clinics may help calibrate those assumptions, provided performance definitions and market conditions are comparable.

For example, a market generating 40,000 modeled annual dermatology visits does not offer 40,000 visits to a new clinic. The relevant question is how much of that resident-generated utilization is addressable after considering current providers, patient travel, payer fit, referrals, the practice’s offering, and realistic operating capacity. Any numerical example in a diligence model should show those steps.

Screen for the next phase of growth without promising whitespace.

A national or regional CurisData file can help an analytics firm identify markets with substantial dermatology demand, favorable growth context, or limited apparent supply. That screen can support acquisition research, de novo planning, or prioritization of markets adjacent to the current footprint.

But a favorable screen is not confirmation of an acquisition target or an underserved market. Provider listings do not establish practice availability or capacity, and demand follows residents rather than facility walls. Market flows, health-system relationships, payer conditions, clinician recruiting, and transaction availability all require separate evidence.

Present the screen as an investigation queue: markets to examine, questions to answer, and reasons a market may move up or down after local validation.

Build a diligence package that exposes the assumptions.

A useful market diligence workstream should connect the investment thesis to inspectable evidence.

  • Existing-footprint assessment comparing clinic performance, patient reach, local demand, competition, and overlap.
  • Expansion-pipeline scorecard using consistent market, access, supply, and network criteria.
  • National or regional opportunity screen identifying markets for further investigation rather than declaring winners.
  • Scenario model separating total resident demand from achievable share, clinic capacity, and ramp assumptions.
  • Risk and validation register documenting missing data, contradictory evidence, source dates, and management questions.
  • Post-close measurement plan defining which assumptions should be revisited as new clinics mature and more operating data becomes available.

CurisData supplies a market input for the diligence team.

CurisData can provide structured specialty users and visits for U.S. block groups, selected markets, or defined service areas. An analytics firm can combine that baseline with the investor’s transaction materials, the practice’s patient and operating data, competitive research, claims, access measures, and its own modeling approach.

The result is not an investment conclusion generated by one dataset. It is a more disciplined test of whether the existing footprint, expansion pipeline, and longer-term market opportunity support the growth story being underwritten.

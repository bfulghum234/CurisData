YOUR GUIDE TO THE ASSESSMENT

Put This Assessment to Work

Turn a population view of specialty demand into a focused conversation about your next market, location, or service.

01

Compare locations

Use estimated annual specialty visits to compare the scale of resident-generated use around candidate sites. Keep geography, source periods, and included services consistent.

02

Explore the specialty mix

Review which specialties generate the most visits, then consider how that mix aligns with your service strategy, referral relationships, and delivery model.

03

Read the maps together

Population shows where residents live. Total visits describe the scale of expected use. Visits per 1,000 residents reveal differences in utilization relative to population size.

04

Add local intelligence

Bring in provider capacity, patient travel, access, referrals, and operating economics. Use these together to evaluate the portion of demand an organization could serve.

Reading the Numbers

Visits include repeat care. Annual visits count encounters, rather than unique patients or people with a condition. A resident can generate visits in several specialties.

Radii overlap. The smaller areas sit within the larger ones. Compare them rather than adding their totals. Whole intersecting block groups extend beyond each circle.

Demand starts with residents. Estimates include care received outside the trade area. Assess achievable volume with explicit assumptions about patient flows, capacity, and capture.

How we develop local estimates

We analyze healthcare survey data weighted to statistically represent the U.S. civilian, noninstitutionalized population. Individual-level records allow us to examine how specialty use varies with age, recorded sex, race and ethnicity, region, and income. The current estimates cover office-based and hospital outpatient physician visits across 25 specialty categories. We apply the modeled relationships to the population profile of a defined geography.

Use the results alongside local evidence. Modeled historical utilization does not directly measure unmet clinical need or predict practice revenue, market share, or site viability.
